# Finding the first restaurant customers

The initial sales plan is to visit multiple restaurants in person and look for owners willing to try SmartMenu on their own menu. The first conversations should test whether the product looks good enough to put on every table and whether an owner would pay to use it.

## First conversation

1. **Put the guest menu on the owner's phone.** Hand them a QR code they can scan. Let them browse the menu, then show one before-and-after dish photo that makes the improvement visible while keeping the dish recognizable.
2. **Offer a preview with their own food.** Ask for a photo of one current menu page and two ordinary phone photos of signature dishes. Use them to make a private mobile preview of one section.
3. **Ask for a paid first setup.** If the owner likes the preview, offer to finish the full menu, provide a QR code, and let staff update prices and availability. Doing the first few setups together can show where the self-service flow needs work.

A possible opener:

> I’m building a digital menu for restaurants. Can I show you what it looks like on your phone? If you like it, send me one page of your current menu and two dish photos. I’ll make a preview so you can see how it would look with your food.

The sample menu supports the sales conversation. The product itself is the working app the restaurant would use to manage its menu.

## Using Google Maps for outreach

Google Maps can help identify restaurants with photos of their current menu or signature dishes. If the restaurant uploaded a photo, ask them to send the original and confirm you can edit it for the preview. If a customer took the photo, get permission from the photographer before copying or editing it. For the reusable sample, use photos you have permission to edit. [Google Maps terms](https://www.google.com/help/terms_maps/) · [Google guidance on photo attribution](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400113?hl=en)
