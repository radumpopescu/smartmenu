# Product vision and workflows

SmartMenu is a self-service web app that restaurants can use to turn their existing paper menus into designed digital menus. The goal is to build a business around a real application that can serve multiple restaurants. The two priorities are ease of use for restaurant staff and an exceptional guest menu.

## Guest experience

- Guests scan a QR code and open the menu directly on their phone, without signing in. The same layout should feel intentional on a tablet.
- The editorial direction uses a strong opening image when available, readable typography, sticky section navigation, and larger placements for signature dishes. Other dishes can use smaller photos or text-only rows.
- The menu should still look good when a restaurant has 50 dishes and only five good photos. Staff should be able to publish from an existing menu and add photography over time.
- Prices and descriptions should be easy to scan at the table. Featured dishes get more visual space so guests can also browse for inspiration.
- The QR code points to a stable URL. Staff can publish changes to prices, availability, or photos without replacing table cards.

## Staff workflow

1. **Photograph the old menu.** Extract sections, dish names, existing descriptions, and prices into an editable draft. Flag unclear entries for review. A side-by-side view with the source page was discussed as a way to make verification easier.
2. **Choose a design.** Start from a coordinated theme or use **Surprise me** to try a new combination of layout, colors, and typography. Keep individual controls available for staff who want to customize the result.
3. **Improve individual photos.** Accept ordinary phone photos and use AI to clean up the background, lighting, crop, or color. Preserve the food, portion, plating, and garnishes so guests recognize what they will actually be served. Show the original and edit side by side and require an explicit choice before using the edit.
4. **Preview and publish.** Show the guest layout during editing. Keep drafts private and publish only when the restaurant is ready to share the result.

## Optional content and design

- Restaurants may want informative descriptions, playful dish names, or a theme with a particular personality. AI-written copy was discussed as an optional draft for staff to review.
- Calories and allergens should come from restaurant-supplied information.
- Both a clean tabletop and a transparent cutout are available in the initial photo-editing workflow. The preferred default remains an open design decision. Try both with real dish photos and check whether the cutout blends into the menu without making the food look artificial.
