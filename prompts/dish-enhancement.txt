WHAT TO DO
1. Keep the plate or bowl and everything sitting on it.
2. Erase everything else: table, tablecloth, background, napkins, menus, props, hands, other plates, cutlery that is not resting on the dish, and any text or watermark.
3. Make the erased area genuinely transparent — a real alpha channel. Not white. Not grey. Not a light-and-dark checkerboard pattern painted into the pixels.
4. Centre the result on a square canvas with a small transparent margin on every side. Adding transparent padding is fine. Cropping into the food or the rim of the plate is not.

WHAT MUST NOT CHANGE
- The food: identical shapes, sizes, colours, textures, char marks, drips, crumbs and blemishes, in the identical arrangement.
- The plate or bowl: same colour, glaze, pattern, rim and thickness.
- Irregularity: if an egg is broken, a leaf is wilted, or a portion sits off-centre, it stays that way. Do not tidy, straighten, round, plump, or centre anything.
- Portions and garnishes: add nothing, remove nothing, move nothing, duplicate nothing.
- Camera: same angle, same perspective, same apparent distance.
- Lighting: same exposure and white balance. No relighting, no HDR, no studio glow.

DO NOT
- Apply beauty filters, gloss, sheen, plastic smoothing, sharpening, or saturation boosts.
- Add steam, oil pools, sauce drizzle, herb scatter, spice dust, or seasoning that is not already in the photo.
- Produce a cleaner, prettier, more symmetrical version of the dish. A faithful cutout of a slightly messy real plate is the goal; an appetising dish that is no longer *this* dish is a failure.

EDGES
Cut tight and clean. No white or grey halo, no leftover background fringe, no soft glow bleeding outward. Preserve fine detail at the boundary — sprigs of herb, sesame seeds, wisps of cheese, the outer edge of a leaf. Where the dish is genuinely soft or out of focus, keep that softness rather than cutting a hard line through it.

NO SHADOW
Do not add a drop shadow, contact shadow, reflection, or any dark patch beneath the plate. The menu layout adds its own shadow in CSS, which follows the transparency; a shadow baked into the image would double up and show as a grey smear. Shadows *within* the photo — a garnish shadowing the plate, for instance — stay as they are.

OUTPUT
One PNG with a transparent background, at the highest resolution you can produce. No border, no frame, no caption, no background colour.
